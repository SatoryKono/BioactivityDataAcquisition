# Research Protocols

This reference provides a lightweight protocol for collecting pillar evidence consistently.

## General Protocol

1. Start from the explicit research questions in `01-pillars/PILLARS.md`.
1. Prefer traceable sources over summaries when possible.
1. Capture the strongest supporting quote, number, or observation for each claim.
1. Record contradictions rather than silently resolving them.
1. Use more than one source type when the question is high impact.

## Pillar Focus Areas

| Pillar        | Focus                                        | Typical Questions                                         |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `market`      | Demand, pricing, segment size, growth        | Is the market large enough? What is willingness to pay?   |
| `users`       | Pain points, workflows, adoption behavior    | Who has the strongest pain? What behaviors repeat?        |
| `tech`        | Feasibility, constraints, architecture risk  | What is hard to build or operate?                         |
| `competitors` | Alternatives, gaps, pricing, positioning     | Where is the current market underserved?                  |
| `design`      | UX constraints, usability, onboarding        | What interaction patterns reduce friction?                |
| `legal`       | Compliance, policy, contracts, data handling | What rules constrain delivery or distribution?            |
| `ops`         | Delivery, support, process, staffing         | What operating model is required?                         |
| `economics`   | Unit economics, margins, acquisition cost    | Does the business model work under realistic assumptions? |

## Source Quality Hints

- Prefer primary documents, direct measurements, or first-hand interviews when available.
- Use secondary sources for orientation, not as the only support for critical claims.
- Treat anonymous, undated, or context-free sources as lower confidence.

## Contradiction Handling

When two sources disagree:

1. Note the contradiction explicitly in both evidence objects.
1. Compare confidence, recency, and source authority.
1. If unresolved, carry both into synthesis and flag the topic for decision-making.
