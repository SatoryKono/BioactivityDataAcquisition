# Pillar Definitions

The default ledger workspace uses eight research pillars.

| Pillar        | Purpose                                                            | Typical Outputs                                                  |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `market`      | Understand demand, pricing, growth, and segment economics          | TAM notes, pricing evidence, segment sizing                      |
| `users`       | Understand user pain points, workflows, and adoption behavior      | interview evidence, behavior patterns, jobs-to-be-done           |
| `tech`        | Understand implementation feasibility and technical constraints    | feasibility notes, architecture risks, dependency constraints    |
| `competitors` | Understand alternatives, positioning, and market gaps              | competitor comparison, benchmark evidence, gap analysis          |
| `design`      | Understand experience, onboarding, usability, and UX trade-offs    | UX evidence, onboarding friction notes, interaction implications |
| `legal`       | Understand compliance, regulatory, policy, and contractual limits  | compliance notes, risk evidence, policy implications             |
| `ops`         | Understand support, delivery, process, staffing, and service model | operating assumptions, support requirements, process constraints |
| `economics`   | Understand unit economics, cost structure, and business viability  | CAC/LTV evidence, margin assumptions, monetization constraints   |

## Default Priority Guidance

- Prioritize `market` and `users` when demand validation is unclear.
- Prioritize `tech` when feasibility or platform constraints dominate.
- Prioritize `competitors` when differentiation is uncertain.
- Keep all remaining pillars active at least at medium priority unless the brief clearly deprioritizes them.
